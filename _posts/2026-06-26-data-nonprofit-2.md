I've decided to develop a kind of "proof of concept" web page for the kind of nonprofit I'm talking about. 
It's very much a work in progress as of this post but it's hosted [here](https://schi-427.github.io/OpenGrandStrand/) with the code being [here](https://github.com/schi-427/OpenGrandStrand).

In designing it I took much inspriration from the (now-inactive) [Sunlight Foundation's website](https://sunlightfoundation.com/). Generally speaking, my idea for a nonprofit could be boiled down
to "The Sunlight Foundation but highly localized". 

For those who aren't familiar, The Sunlight Foundation was a national nonprofit based in Washington DC that advocated for Open Government principles. Some examples for their [initiatives](https://sunlightfoundation.com/our-work/) included:

- Federal website change monitoring
- Presidential conflict of interest identification and tracking
- Public Data management
- Strengthening FOIA
- Strengthening Federal Open Data policies
- State and local-level open data policies
- The Open Cities Initiative

The Open Cities Initiative seems especially relevant to my interests, as this was geared towards making "municipal government acrosss the country more transparent, accountable, and participatory." 
Specifically this included pushing the concepts of community-centered open data, open contracting (a new concept to me but very interesting in my view), and ensuring smart city technology accounts for the public's digital rights. 

In my view, local change is best advocated for at the local level, when initiated by local stakeholders. National nonprofits are important of course, but I feel local residents are in the best position to advocate for initiatives centered around local policies and systems. 
As an example, if we're advocating for the implementation of open data portals in local cities, this would naturally involve inidividualized plans. City A may already have internal digital datasets but simply lack a dedicated public portal for open data. City B meanwhile might lack any kind of data infrastructure. 
It would be unhelpful then to approach City A and City B in the same way, but rather account for their particular present conditions at the beginning. This is best done, I think, with local residents engaging local public officials. This is my rationale for keeping the focus of my potential nonprofit on regional governments and bodies. 

Anyway, in terms of the site architecture, I've loved using Vue.JS for many years now and it seemed to be a natural fit for my purposes. It's in the early phases of development but I think I've got a good foundation to work off of. Will post more updates as I continue working on the project.
