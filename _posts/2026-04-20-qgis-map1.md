# My Greenspaces Project

I've been working on a project centered around assessing the greenspace accessibility in my town. 
This idea was rooted in noticing that my area's population and consequent housing development have increased significantly over the past few years, and I began to wonder whether these new housing developments had adequate access to Greenspaces.
Since then I've been reading different research articles centered around analyzing greenspace access, such as:

- [Beyond the Backyard: GIS Analysis of Public Green Space Accessibility in Australian Metropolitan Areas ](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/8/4694)
- [Measuring the accessibility and equity of public parks: A case study using GIS](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/35504885_Measuring_the_accessibility_and_equity_of_public_parks_A_case_study_using_GIS)
- [Analysis of urban green space accessibility and distribution inequity in the City of Chicago](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1618866721000546)
- [Accessibility to urban green spaces: A critical review of WHO recommendations in the light of tree-covered areas assessment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X24010057)
- [Spatial accessibility analysis of green space from a health-benefit perspective: Implications for healthy urban development](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2022.1083563/full)
- [Study on the Accessibility of Urban Parks Within the Framework of Kunming’s 15-Min Living Circle ](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-445X/14/5/933)
- [Access to urban parks: Comparing spatial accessibility measures using three GIS-based approaches](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0198971521001204)
- [Accessibility to greenspaces: GIS based indicators for sustainable planning in a dense urban context](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X13004299)
- [Using a GIS-based network analysis to determine urban greenspace accessibility for different ethnic and religious groups](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169204608000066)
- [Modeling spatial accessibility to parks: a national study ](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21554690/)
- [Review of the impact of urban parks and green spaces on residence prices in the environmental health context](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.993801/full)

I've set up a web map page for my project, including my major map layers so far, here: https://schi-427.github.io/horry-county-gis/#12/33.8573/-79.0094 

My data sources generally consist of official government websites, such as the Horry County and City of Cownay GIS pages. The census blocks data comes from the US Census' Arcgis page: https://hub.arcgis.com/datasets/d795eaa6ee7a40bdb2efeb2d001bf823_0/about

I am still working on researching the best methods for answering my core questions, which are:
- Is there a relationship between housing development age and greenspace access in Conway?
- How accessible are greenspaces to Conway residents?

While I have the census blocks data that includes population, this data is from the last census which was in 2020, so Im not sure how informative this will ultimately be. 
Also, the census blocks data, while possibly useful, does not answer my questions regarding housing development recency. 
My current approach for that is to select for land parcels (provided by county, includes parcel sell date, a possible proxy for development age?) that fall within Conway city limits (possibly further refined by residential zones), and then running some kind of proximity analysis or regression so that I can get the relationship between parcel sell date and greenspace access. 

So yeah, still tinkering with the specifics here, but I think continuing to read the related literature will help me refine my approach. 
Writing this now, I think it's clear that one of my main hurdles is defining what greenspace access entails. 
The [Beyond the Backyard](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/8/4694) study (first one in list above) lays out many potential metrics, so ultimately what I might do is create a list of formulas and adapt them as appropriate to fit my available data types.

Another consideration I need to make: how do I handle certain spaces like the blue ways trails that are water-based or bike trails, where access is limited by a person's access to boats or bikes respectively? 

Overall Im excited about this work and think it could provide a lot of valuable insight for City residents and decision-makers, and might be readily applied to other cities in the county. 

I think an app that applies my methodology here to any given city in the country is feasible, though obviously much more complex in scope. For that I would need a reliable way to dynamically gather greenspace data from many different endpoints which just might not be realistic at this time. If all of that data was just in ArcGIS that would be one thing, but generally it seems like it's held in individual city/government websites.
